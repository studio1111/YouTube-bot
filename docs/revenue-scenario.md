# Channel revenue scenario

## Verdict

Do **not** build this as an ad-revenue-only channel. The strongest plan is a three-layer funnel: free videos build trust, a small digital product or service creates the first meaningful revenue, and YouTube monetization compounds later. Automation should reduce production cost, not turn the channel into repetitive AI spam.

This plan assumes one English-language science/technology channel, 2 long-form videos and 5 Shorts per week, a human reviewer, and local-first tooling. All figures below are planning assumptions, not promises.

## 1. Offer and audience

**Positioning:** complex science and technology explained through surprising stories, visual demonstrations, and practical implications.

**Audience:** curious students, builders, technical professionals, and lifelong learners.

**Primary promise:** one clear idea, one memorable story, one useful takeaway per episode.

**Initial offer ladder:**

1. Free: long-form explainers and Shorts.
2. Email list: weekly research brief and source pack.
3. Low-ticket: downloadable visual explainers, study packs, prompt packs, or project templates at $9-$19.
4. Membership: monthly behind-the-scenes research, source notes, live Q&A, and early access at $4.99-$9.99/month.
5. Sponsorship: only products that fit the audience, with clear disclosure.
6. Higher-ticket: workshop, consulting, or custom research package after audience trust exists.

Do not launch the paid offer before the channel has repeated evidence of what viewers ask for. Start with a waitlist and validate demand in comments and email replies.

## 2. Funnel and weekly operating model

```text
Shorts / search discovery
        ↓
Long-form episode
        ↓
Pinned source pack + email opt-in
        ↓
Low-ticket product or membership
        ↓
Sponsor / affiliate / workshop
        ↓
Analytics feedback into the next topic
```

Weekly cadence:

- 2 long-form videos: 6-10 minutes, original argument and sources.
- 5 Shorts: hooks extracted from the long-form research, not copy-pasted slideshows.
- 1 community post or live Q&A.
- 1 email with sources, corrections, and the next question being investigated.

The agent may automate research collection, drafts, asset manifests, captions, SEO, rendering, and reporting. A human must approve factual claims, rights, final media, sponsor fit, and public release.

## 3. Revenue phases

### Phase 0: validation, months 0-2

**Goal:** prove that people return for the topic and format, not just one lucky Short.

Targets:

- 16-20 long-form videos.
- 40-50 Shorts.
- 500 email subscribers or qualified waitlist signups.
- 3 repeatable topic clusters.
- 10 audience interviews or structured comment reviews.

Revenue target: **$0-$100/month**. Spend: software $0 by default; hosting, electricity, and optional hardware are excluded from the “free software” claim.

Gate to continue: at least 20% of long-form viewers reach the midpoint, and at least 10% of returning viewers watch another episode within 30 days. Replace these with channel-specific baselines once enough data exists.

### Phase 1: first owned revenue, months 3-6

Launch one product based on repeated audience questions:

- $12 average price.
- 1,000 email subscribers.
- 1.5% monthly buyer conversion from the list.

Illustrative monthly revenue: **15 buyers × $12 = $180 gross**.

Add affiliate links only for tools or books genuinely used in the episode. Never let affiliate commission choose the conclusion.

### Phase 2: diversified revenue, months 6-12

Illustrative monthly base case:

| Stream | Assumption | Gross/month |
|---|---:|---:|
| YouTube ads/Premium | 80,000 monetized long-form views, $4 RPM planning assumption | $320 |
| Digital product | 40 sales × $15 | $600 |
| Membership | 80 members × $6 | $480 |
| Affiliate | 25 qualified conversions × $8 commission | $200 |
| Sponsorship | 1 small, relevant integration | $500 |
| **Total** |  | **$2,100** |

This is a scenario, not a forecast. RPM, sponsor rates, conversion rates, taxes, refunds, platform fees, and country availability can move the result sharply.

### Phase 3: year 2 operating model

Only add a second channel, localization, or a larger product after the first channel has:

- 6 consecutive months of positive contribution margin;
- a documented rights/provenance process;
- a stable human review queue;
- fewer than 5% failed renders or broken deliveries;
- repeatable topic clusters with returning viewers.

The best expansion is usually localization of proven episodes, not a second generic channel.

## 4. YouTube eligibility and policy gates

YouTube says the expanded YPP entry path can be available at **500 subscribers, 3 valid public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 qualified public watch hours in 12 months or 3 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days**, where the expanded program is available. Full ads/Premium eligibility is currently described as **1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 qualified public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days**. Check the channel's actual Earn tab and country availability before planning around these numbers.

YouTube has also published a scheduled change stating that starting **February 1, 2027**, new creators entering through ads/Premium will need **8,000 qualified watch hours or 20 million qualified Shorts views**, while fan funding and Shopping thresholds remain unchanged. Treat this as a planning risk and verify the official policy before launch.

Most important policy risk: YouTube expects original, authentic content and can reject mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative channels from monetization. The agent must add original research, editorial judgment, meaningful narration, and real variation between episodes. A text-to-speech slideshow factory is a bad business model.

## 5. Unit economics

Track contribution margin per episode:

```text
episode contribution = attributable revenue - compute - storage - music/media rights - editing time - refunds/fees
```

Suggested internal targets:

- Local software/API cost per long-form draft: $0-$5.
- Human review budget: 45-90 minutes per long-form episode.
- Render failure rate: <5%.
- Rights ledger completeness: 100% of external assets.
- Correction rate after publication: <3% of factual claims.
- Email opt-in conversion from qualified viewers: 1%-3% initially.
- Product refund rate: <8%.

The real constraint is not API price. It is attention, trust, review time, and the cost of fixing a public mistake.

## 6. Revenue experiments to run in order

1. **Topic test:** publish three angles on one subject and compare returning viewers, midpoint retention, saves, and comments, not just CTR.
2. **Lead magnet test:** source pack vs. checklist vs. project template.
3. **Offer test:** $9 vs. $15 product with the same promise.
4. **Membership test:** early access vs. monthly live Q&A.
5. **Sponsor test:** only after the channel has a stable audience profile and a one-page media kit.

Declare a winner only after a pre-set sample threshold. Do not optimize on a single viral outlier.

## 7. Dashboard fields the agent should track

- Views, watch time, average percentage viewed, returning viewers.
- Impressions, CTR, traffic source, and retention by opening hook.
- Email views, opt-ins, click-through, and unsubscribe rate.
- Product visits, conversion, refunds, and net revenue.
- Membership starts, cancellations, and active members.
- Affiliate clicks, qualified conversions, and commission.
- Sponsor revenue, delivered impressions, and disclosure status.
- Cost and minutes per episode.
- Rights/provenance completeness and correction incidents.

## 8. Stop rules

Pause monetization experiments and review the workflow if:

- a video receives a copyright or policy claim;
- a source cannot be verified;
- synthetic media disclosure is unclear;
- the format becomes materially repetitive;
- the product is being promoted without evidence of audience need;
- sponsorship would compromise the editorial conclusion.
